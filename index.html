PM2.5 Outdoor Exposure

Haze exposure calculator

Estimates how much PM2.5 you breathe in on walks, commutes and exercise, using official hourly readings from Singapore's National Environment Agency (NEA, via data.gov.sg), and shows it as a share of your daily PM2.5 budget at the WHO guideline.

1 · PM2.5 readings for the day

Each box is the average PM2.5 for that hour of the day, as measured by NEA. You can overwrite any value.

2 · You and your outdoor trips

Age sets how much air you breathe. Health status sets which NEA advice applies to you.

3 · Your outdoor exposure

Your daily PM2.5 budget is what you would breathe in over 24 hours if the air stayed at the WHO guideline of 15 µg/m³. It resets each day.

This counts outdoor time only. Indoor air uses part of the same daily budget and will be added in a future version.

Method. For each trip: inhaled PM2.5 (µg) = hourly PM2.5 (µg/m³) × breathing rate (m³/h) × hours × (1 − mask filtration). Breathing rates by age and activity are the means from the US EPA Exposure Factors Handbook 2011, Table 6-2 (standing = sedentary, walking = light, brisk walk or cycling = moderate, jogging = high intensity), averaged across the handbook's age bins. The daily budget is 15 µg/m³ × daily breathing volume for your age (same handbook, Table 6-1).

Exposure levels are set at the WHO 2021 24-hour guideline (15 µg/m³) and its interim targets, expressed as a share of the daily budget: up to 100% = 15 · 167% = 25 · 250% = 37.5 (also Singapore's 24-hour target) · 333% = 50 · 500% = 75.

Risk level uses the highest hourly reading you were out in, against NEA's 1-hour PM2.5 bands (I: 0 to 55, II: 56 to 150, III: 151 to 250, IV: 251 and above), and follows the NEA/MOH personal guide (July 2024), which gives vulnerable persons stricter advice at the same band. Healthy: Band I Low, II Moderate, III High, IV Very high. Vulnerable: Band I Low, II High, III and IV Very high. Vulnerable persons are the elderly, pregnant women, children, and people with chronic lung or heart disease; here, under-13s and over-65s count as children and elderly.

Masks. Filtration figures include typical fit leakage and are practical estimates, not certified values: cloth ≈50%, surgical ≈65%, KN95 ≈80%, N95 ≈90%.

Sources. US EPA Exposure Factors Handbook, Ch. 6 · WHO Global Air Quality Guidelines (2021) · NEA haze advisories · PM2.5 readings: National Environment Agency via data.gov.sg, under the Singapore Open Data Licence.
